## Week by week

### Week 1 - Working with Others
Week subtitle: Professional Roles of Nursing, Nursing Education, Nursing Organizations, and
Interprofessionalism. Those four topics are exactly the three assigned chapters.

**Outcomes.** The week page publishes CSLO A and B. Its "Module Learning Outcomes" heading is
followed by a reading list, not outcomes, so the working outcomes come from the assigned decks:

- Chapter 1: describe the demographic profile of registered nurses; recognize the range of settings
  and roles; identify evolving practice opportunities; consider nursing roles in various practice
  settings; explain the roles and education of advanced practice nurses.
- Chapter 12: differentiate social from professional relationships; explain professional
  boundaries; describe therapeutic use of self; identify key aspects of collaboration.
- Chapter 15: explain why professions have associations; describe the role associations play in the
  profession and in society; recognize the leadership opportunities associations offer.

**Materials.** Black (2024) chapters 1, 12, and 15 with publisher decks, all three heavyweight.
Plus fourteen videos on the Knowledge page across e-portfolio, capstone, service learning, quality
improvement, and mentoring and coaching.

What the three chapters turn on:

- Chapter 1: hospital against every other setting; roles open at entry against roles needing added
  education or experience; the four advanced practice roles and the graduate preparation each takes.
- Chapter 12: a social relationship against a professional one; the continuum running
  under-involvement, zone of helpfulness, over-involvement.
- Chapter 15: politics against policy; associations enrolling individual nurses against a federation
  whose members are national associations; citizen, activist, and politician levels of involvement.

**Takeaway.** Nursing practice reaches well past the hospital bedside, and which parts of it a
learner can reach depends on preparation. Getting anywhere in it runs through relationships with
other professionals that have to be deliberately bounded, and through associations that carry the
profession's interests collectively.

**Graded work.** Discussion #1 on Professional Organization Leadership, cognitive. Initial Journal,
Preceptor Agreement, and Time & Attendance Log, mixed: the log and agreement are administrative and
the plan and Benner reflection are cognitive. Ice Breaker, CastleBranch Verification, and the
syllabus acknowledgment, all administrative.

- **Main. Choose Your Practicum Ground.** Quadrant Grid. Step 1 places eight nursing roles on a
  grid of hospital against non-hospital by whether a new BSN graduate can hold the role, serving
  the settings, roles, and advanced-practice outcomes from Chapter 1. Step 2 matches the four
  advanced practice roles to their scope, serving the roles and education outcome. Step 3 judges
  what a telehealth role adds beyond clinical skill, serving the evolving opportunities outcome.
  Step 4 places a drifting preceptor relationship on the professional behavior continuum, serving
  Chapter 12's boundaries and social-versus-professional outcomes and CSLO A. Step 5 drafts the
  setting, nurse expert, and area of interest.
  Framing: a professional question, no cast. The learner classifies roles against criteria and the
  criteria are the content, so invented people would only compete for attention.
  Ties to: the plans for course completion section of the Initial Journal.
- **Mini. Which Organization Carries This Fight.** Evidence Match.
  Focus material: Black (2024) Chapter 15, Political Activation in Nursing.
  Framing: a full scenario with two named nurses, because the goals being routed belong to them and
  one of them asks the question in step 2.
  Why: Discussion #1 asks students to compare the ANA, an NCEMNA member organization, and the ICN
  by mission, membership, and president. Chapter 15 teaches that a mission statement is generated
  by the membership and defines purpose and who the organization serves, which is the reasoning the
  discussion needs.
- **Third file. Practicum Hours Planner.** Lever Simulator, on the Practicum Overview table. The
  90 hour log is a real administrative deliverable due this week and it is easy to get wrong, so it
  is rehearsed here rather than as the main interactive.
  Framing: a full scenario with the preceptor and the faculty coordinator, because both speak and
  the scheduling consequence in step 3 is theirs to approve.

**Coverage.** Chapter 1 and Chapter 12 in the main, Chapter 15 in the mini, the Practicum Overview
table in the third file. Chapter 1's demographic profile outcome is touched only in a feedback
line. The fourteen Knowledge page videos are not represented: the e-portfolio, capstone, service
learning, and quality improvement sets are previews of Weeks 5 through 7 and are covered in those
weeks, but the two mentoring and coaching videos support CSLO A this week and are still uncovered.

### Week 2 - Quality Improvement, Service-Learning, and Collaborative Relationships
Tags: (CSLO A, E)

- **Main. Service or Experience.** Two-Bin Sort. Sort community activities into service learning
  or experiential learning, then repair a vague goal into a SMART goal and choose the evidence-based
  strategies that would carry the project. Ends in drafting the comparison paragraph.
  Ties to: Discussion #2 on Service Learning.
- **Mini. Which Dimension Just Failed.** Triage Routing.
  Focus material: "The Six Dimensions of Healthcare Quality" video.
  Why: Week 2 carries the course's first quality-improvement content, and CSLO E has students
  create a project to solve a clinical health issue. Naming which dimension of quality a problem
  violates is the move the capstone project depends on all semester.

### Week 3 - PICO, KSVME, and QI Project Proposal
Tags: (CSLO D, E)

- **Main. Appraise the Evidence.** Red Flag Hunt. Three candidate articles for a unit's practice
  problem. Find what disqualifies each one across Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, and
  Purpose, then judge Applicability and Feasibility for the learner's own workplace. Ends in
  drafting one full annotation.
  Ties to: Assignment #1 Annotated Bibliography.
- **Mini. Novice to Expert.** Process Chain.
  Focus material: Benner, P. (2023). Patricia Benner's Novice to Expert Theory in Nursing.
  Why: Both the initial journal and the mid-term journal require a self-assessment against Benner's
  stages, so this video is the single material the graded journals visibly depend on.

### Week 4 - Midterm, Portfolio Progress, Quality Improvement and Service-Learning Updates
Tags: (CSLO A-F)

- **Main. From Problem to PICOT.** Branching Consult. Choose one of three practice problems on a
  named unit. The choice changes what the comparison, outcome, and measurement steps ask, and a
  weak choice shows its cost. Ends in drafting the PICOT statement and one SMART goal.
  Ties to: Assignment #2, Literature Review on Quality Improvement Project.
- **Mini. The Vocabulary of Clinical Judgment.** Terminology Crossword.
  Focus material: Black (2024) Chapter 11, Developing Nursing Judgment Through Critical Thinking.
  Why: Chapter 11 is the week's anchor chapter and carries the prioritization frameworks. It also
  sits on the midterm's concept territory without rehearsing exam items.
- **Also needed here.** Chapter 1, assigned in Week 1, is only ever assessed on this week's
  midterm, and its content is recognition-level workforce, settings, and roles material. Its
  demographic profile outcome is best served as midterm preparation in this week rather than in
  Week 1.

### Week 5 - E-Portfolio and Lifelong Learning
Tags: (CSLO E)

- **Main. The Hiring Panel.** Stakeholder Hot Seat. Three named panelists interview the learner for
  a hypothetical position. Each question can only be answered with the right portfolio component,
  and a thin answer draws a follow-up. Ends in drafting the cover letter opening and one five-year
  goal.
  Ties to: Electronic Portfolio.
- **Mini. Pick Your Model.** Evidence Match.
  Focus material: Black (2024) Chapter 10, The Science of Nursing and Evidence-Based Practice.
  Why: Discussion #3 names the IOWA Model, the Johns Hopkins model, and the ACE Star Model, but no
  course material teaches them. See the gap list below. Chapter 10 is the closest course-owned
  source and this check stays on what Chapter 10 actually covers.

### Week 6 - Evidence-Based Practice, Lifelong Learning, and Service Learning
Tags: (CSLO A-F)

- **Main. Your Research Journey to 2030.** Timeline Build. Place career and research milestones on
  a 2026 to 2030 track, discover which ones cannot come before others, and see what a plan with no
  preparation steps looks like. Ends in drafting the research-journey narrative.
  Ties to: Discussion #4, Lifelong Learning.
- **Mini. Equity or Equality.** Two-Bin Sort.
  Focus material: The Future of Nursing 2020-2030 (2021), Chapter 5, The Role of Nurses in
  Improving Health Equity.
  Why: Chapter 5 is the assigned reading that feeds both Discussion #4 and the service-learning
  project, and the equity and equality distinction is the one the reading turns on.

### Week 7 - Quality Improvement Capstone Project
Tags: (CSLO A-F)

- **Main. Stress Test Your Intervention.** Case Stress Test. Run one improvement intervention
  against four different units and watch where the plan holds and where it breaks, then choose the
  measure that would actually detect the change. Ends in drafting the rationale and measurement
  section.
  Ties to: Final Quality Improvement Capstone Project.
- **Mini. Run the Improvement Cycle.** Process Chain.
  Focus material: Stratis Health (2023). QI Basics, Introduction to Quality Improvement, Processes
  and Systems.
  Why: Week 7 has no Knowledge page of its own. See the gap list below. This video is in the
  course's own media library on the Week 1 Knowledge page and the Refresher folder, and it is the
  only course material that walks through an improvement cycle end to end.

### Week 8 - Closing
Tags: none published on the week page

- **Main. What the Practicum Taught You.** Evidence Match. Match practicum moments to the KSVME
  cornerstone and the course outcome each one evidenced, then see which outcomes have no evidence
  behind them. Ends in drafting the letter of appreciation to the preceptor.
  Ties to: Letter of Appreciation to your Preceptor, Final Journal.
- **No mini check.** Week 8 has no readings or resources, so there is no focus material to build
  one on. One file only, rather than padding.

## Gaps found in the course, for the instructor

These are reported, not filled. Nothing below was invented into an interactive.

1. **Week 5 has no new instructional materials.** The Knowledge page holds midterm logistics only,
   yet the week carries two graded items, the Electronic Portfolio and Discussion #3.
2. **Discussion #3 names three EBP models the course never teaches.** The IOWA Model, the Johns
   Hopkins model, and the ACE Star Model appear in the prompt with no reading, video, or handout
   behind them.
3. **Week 7 has no Knowledge (Readings and Resources) page**, although it carries the capstone
   project, the final journal, and the final attendance log.
4. **Week 8 has no readings and no outcomes published** on the week page.
5. **No MLO codes exist anywhere in the course.** The Week 1 page labels a list of textbook
   chapters as "Module Learning Outcomes." Every interactive therefore tags CSLO letters.
6. **Week 5's page publishes only CSLO E**, but its Electronic Portfolio deliverable is the course's
   outcome F work. The Week 5 tag above follows the published page.
7. **Stale dates inside live week pages.** Week 4 and Week 5 pages reference 11/18/25 and 4/8/25,
   the capstone assignment text references January 31, 2023, and the service-learning presentation
   text references May 1, 2023. The Spring 2026 schedule says otherwise. No interactive carries a
   date.
8. **The export contains a duplicate eight-week template shell** titled "[Insert Title of Module
   and Date]" alongside the live dated weeks. An import would look like two courses.
9. **Benner is required in Week 1 but taught in Week 2.** The Initial Journal is due Wednesday of
   Week 1 and asks for a self-assessment against Benner's novice to expert stages. Benner's stages
   are taught in Black Chapter 5, which is Week 2's reading, revisited in Chapter 11 in Week 4, and
   the Benner video sits on the Week 3 Knowledge page. No Week 1 material introduces the framework.
10. **Gradebook residual from the template shell.** `Exemplar Weekly Quiz` carries a 100 point
    gradebook column but belongs to the `[Insert Title of Module and Date]` shell and is not a
    course assessment. Delete the column.
11. **Duplicated gradebook columns that would double-count.** `Electronic Portfolio` and
    `Final Quality Improvement Capstone Project` each appear twice, and the final journal and
    attendance log appear as three overlapping columns: `Final Journal`,
    `Final Journal and Time & Attendance Log`, and
    `Time and Attendance Log - Final Submission of Total Clinical Hours`. The two service-learning
    columns also carry different names for what the week pages present as one deliverable.
12. **The syllabus acknowledgment has no gradebook column.** The Week 1 page requires it by
    Tuesday, so it needs a weight and a column, or the week page should stop presenting it as a
    requirement.
13. **The Practicum Overview table does not add up.** On the Week 1 Practicum Documents page, the
   five weekly categories come to 14.5 hours a week, which is 87 hours across six weeks, but the
   row labelled Total reads 90 hours and the course requires 90. Either a category's weekly figure
   is understated or three hours are unassigned. The Week 1 interactive teaches the published
   per-category allocation and tells the learner to confirm the remaining three hours with the
   faculty coordinator, because correcting the table is the instructor's call.
